[Example Math Problem]
Solve for $r$: \[\frac{r-45}{2} = \frac{3-2r}{5}.\]

[Example Solution]
<paragraph_0>
## Step 1: Multiply both sides of the equation by 10 to clear the denominators.
To get rid of the fractions, we multiply both sides by the least common multiple of the denominators, which is 10.
</paragraph_0>

<paragraph_1>
## Step 2: Distribute and simplify the equation.
Multiplying both sides by 10, we get \[5(r-45) = 2(3-2r).\] This simplifies to \[5r - 225 = 6 - 4r.\]
</paragraph_1>

<paragraph_2>
## Step 3: Get all the terms with r on one side of the equation and the constants on the other side.
Moving the terms involving $r$ to one side and the constants to the other side, we have \[5r + 4r = 6 + 225.\]
</paragraph_2>

<paragraph_3>
## Step 4: Combine like terms and solve for r.
Combining like terms and solving for $r$, we have \[9r = 231.\] Dividing both sides by 9, we get \[r = \frac{231}{9} = \boxed{23.66666667}.\]
</paragraph_3>

<paragraph_4>
Therefore, the final answer is: $\boxed{23.66666667}$
</paragraph_4>

[Example Judgment]
We go through the solution paragraph by paragraph and check whether each paragraph is still valid given the previous ones.

Paragraph 0 is correct. The original equation is
\[
\frac{r-45}{2} = \frac{3-2r}{5}.
\]
The denominators are 2 and 5, so multiplying both sides by their least common multiple, 10, is a valid way to clear the fractions.

Paragraph 1 is correct. After multiplying both sides by 10, we get
\[
10 \cdot \frac{r-45}{2} = 10 \cdot \frac{3-2r}{5},
\]
which becomes
\[
5(r-45) = 2(3-2r).
\]
Distributing gives
\[
5r - 225 = 6 - 4r,
\]
so the algebra in this paragraph is correct.

Paragraph 2 is also correct. Starting from
\[
5r - 225 = 6 - 4r,
\]
adding \(4r\) to both sides gives
\[
5r + 4r - 225 = 6,
\]
and then adding 225 to both sides gives
\[
5r + 4r = 6 + 225.
\]
So although the paragraph writes the condensed result directly, it is still a valid algebraic transformation.

Paragraph 3 is the first incorrect paragraph. Combining like terms to get
\[
9r = 231
\]
is correct. However, dividing by 9 is done incorrectly:
\[
\frac{231}{9} = \frac{77}{3} = 25.666666\ldots,
\]
not \(23.66666667\). So the reasoning first becomes invalid here.

Paragraph 4 is also incorrect because it depends on paragraph 3 and repeats the same wrong final answer. Once paragraph 3 is wrong, this paragraph must also be labeled 0.

[Example Math Problem]
Let $b$ be a number such that $(2b+5)(b-1)=6b.$ What is the largest possible value of $b$? Express your answer as a common fraction.

[Example Solution]
<paragraph_0>
## Step 1: Expand the left side of the equation
We start by expanding the left side of the given equation: $(2b+5)(b-1)=6b$. This yields $2b^2 + 5b - 2b - 5 = 6b$.
</paragraph_0>

<paragraph_1>
## Step 2: Simplify the equation
Combine like terms to simplify the equation: $2b^2 + 3b - 5 = 6b$.
</paragraph_1>

<paragraph_2>
## Step 3: Rearrange the equation
Rearrange the equation so that it equals zero: $2b^2 - 3b - 5 = 0$.
</paragraph_2>

<paragraph_3>
## Step 4: Solve the quadratic equation
We can solve this quadratic equation by factoring or using the quadratic formula. Unfortunately, this quadratic does not factor nicely, so we use the quadratic formula: $b = \frac{-(-3) \pm \sqrt{(-3)^2 - 4(2)(-5)}}{2(2)}$. Simplify inside the square root: $b = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 40}}{4}$. Continue simplifying: $b = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{49}}{4}$.
</paragraph_3>

<paragraph_4>
## Step 5: Simplify further
Simplify the square root: $b = \frac{3 \pm 7}{4}$. Now solve for both values of b: $b = \frac{3 + 7}{4}$ or $b = \frac{3 - 7}{4}$. These yield $b = \frac{10}{4}$ or $b = \frac{-4}{4}$. Simplify further to get $b = \frac{5}{2}$ or $b = -1$.
</paragraph_4>

<paragraph_5>
## Step 6: Determine the largest possible value of b
Since we need the largest possible value of b, compare the two solutions: $\frac{5}{2}$ is greater than -1.
</paragraph_5>

<paragraph_6>
Therefore, the final answer is: $\boxed{\frac{5}{2}}$.
</paragraph_6>

[Example Judgment]
We again verify the solution paragraph by paragraph.

Paragraph 0 is correct. Expanding
\[
(2b+5)(b-1)
\]
gives
\[
2b\cdot b + 2b\cdot (-1) + 5\cdot b + 5\cdot (-1)=2b^2-2b+5b-5=2b^2+5b-2b-5,
\]
so the expansion in the paragraph is valid.

Paragraph 1 is correct. Combining like terms in
\[
2b^2 + 5b - 2b - 5 = 6b
\]
gives
\[
2b^2 + 3b - 5 = 6b.
\]
This simplification is correct.

Paragraph 2 is correct. Subtracting \(6b\) from both sides gives
\[
2b^2 + 3b - 5 - 6b = 0,
\]
which simplifies to
\[
2b^2 - 3b - 5 = 0.
\]
So this rearrangement is valid.

Paragraph 3 is correct. For
\[
2b^2 - 3b - 5 = 0,
\]
the quadratic formula gives
\[
b=\frac{-(-3)\pm\sqrt{(-3)^2-4(2)(-5)}}{2\cdot 2}
=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{9+40}}{4}
=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{49}}{4}.
\]
Each substitution is correct.

Paragraph 4 is correct. Since \(\sqrt{49}=7\), we get
\[
b=\frac{3\pm 7}{4}.
\]
This gives the two solutions
\[
\frac{3+7}{4}=\frac{10}{4}=\frac{5}{2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{3-7}{4}=\frac{-4}{4}=-1.
\]
So the simplification in this paragraph is correct.

Paragraph 5 is correct. Comparing the two values, \(\frac{5}{2} > -1\), so the largest possible value of \(b\) is \(\frac{5}{2}\).

Paragraph 6 is correct because it states the same correct final answer obtained in the previous paragraph.

Since every paragraph is valid and the final answer is correct, all step labels are 1 and the final label is 1.

```json
{
  "step_labels": {"0": 1, "1": 1, "2": 1, "3": 1, "4": 1, "5": 1, "6": 1},
  "final_label": 1
}
```
